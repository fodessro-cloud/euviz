What the rates mean

  • ECB deposit rate: the interest the European Central Bank pays banks on their overnight deposits. It is the ECB's key rate: Euribor and deposit rates follow it, Euribor also prices in where markets expect it to go.
  • Euribor: the rate at which banks in the euro area lend to each other for 1, 3, 6 or 12 months. Variable mortgages and many loans follow it, and banks price term deposits off it.
  • Government bonds: the yield of euro area government bonds with 2, 5 and 10 years left, all euro area governments together (ECB yield curve).
  • AAA government bonds: the same for the governments with the best credit rating only, such as Germany and the Netherlands.
  • Corporate bonds: the average yield of bonds issued by German companies other than banks (Bundesbank). It is the only free official series for company bonds in euro; European indices of corporate bonds are sold under licence.
  • Term deposit: the average rate banks paid households on new deposits fixed for up to one year in the euro area. The best offers on the market are higher.
